THE LION FORUM INSTITUTE EST. 2026 PROSPECTUS EST. 2026 LION FORUM INSTITUTE The Lion Forum Institute exists to ensure the technologies of tomorrow strengthen American families today. OUR WHY The American Dream is fraying. The pillars that once made middle-class life attainable – affordable health care, quality education, competitive work – have eroded under decades of rising costs and stagnant wages. This is the great challenge of our time. That challenge will require a multifaceted response, and technology has a central role to play. Throughout history, tech has propelled American families forward. Electricity transformed household labor and hygiene, penicillin eradicated disease, and the internet connected entrepreneurs from Mississippi to New York City. We are now living through another great leap: the dawn of artificial intelligence. It has the power to ease the mental health crisis, give every child a world-class tutor, and power a future with abundant housing and clean energy. But it’s not guaranteed that these benefits will reach the median American family. Many of America’s most important institutions lack the capacity and strategic plan to shape these tools around the needs of all families. And trust has collapsed: skepticism of technology, and of the people building it, runs rampant. The risk is that this hardens into a politics that rejects technological progress altogether. The Lion Forum Institute exists to shape emerging technologies to reinforce the American Dream and to prove that these innovations can transform health, education, and work for all families. WHAT WE DO The Lion Forum Institute leads high-impact pilots that shape cutting-edge technologies around the core needs of American families. We work on the critical issues at the center of American life – healthcare, education, workforce, and energy – and bring together the stakeholders needed to solve them: leading founders and technology companies, universities and health systems, community partners, and local, state, and federal government. For each pilot, we identify a problem, match it with a technology partner, recruit implementation partners, and measure outcomes. Our pilots are designed to show measurable results in 3 to 12 months. Each pilot demonstrates, through its own specific use case, how tech can improve everyday life for the median family in the ways that matter most. We then share those lessons with the world. THE MODEL LFI Core Partners make multi-year commitments that both build the Institute and fund the pilots they choose to support. A typical $2 million annual Core Partnership is structured as: $1 million CORE SUPPORT Supports LFI’s team, technology, and infrastructure required to develop the pipeline of high-impact pilots. $1 million DEDICATED PILOT FUNDING Deployed across pilot opportunities the partner selects to support. Partnership levels scale with the size of the annual commitment, with our largest Core Partners receiving first look across LFI’s full pipeline of new pilots. CURRENT PILOT PIPELINE FOUR ACTIVE PILOTS — 2026 01 Special Olympics: Global Health Data Problem: Special Olympics Healthy Athletes has delivered more than 2 million free health screenings since 1997, creating one of the largest health access programs for people with intellectual and developmental disabilities. The next challenge is helping athletes and caregivers turn screenings into better follow-up care. Approach: The Institute is working with Special Olympics to gather health data from Healthy Athletes screenings across the world and develop an open-source dataset that researchers, health systems, and technology partners can build on. Goal: Improve the quality of healthcare for people with intellectual and developmental disabilities globally, and make the world’s largest IDD health dataset an open resource. 02 Governor Inslee: Clean Data Center Playbook Problem: Data centers are becoming critical infrastructure, but states need better tools to negotiate projects that create jobs, protect communities, and support clean energy goals. Approach: The Institute is working with Governor Inslee, one of the country’s leading clean energy policymakers, to develop a practical playbook for states negotiating with data center developers. The playbook will focus on clean power, energy affordability, workforce development, permitting, and community benefit. Goal: Help states build data centers quickly in a way that expands clean energy, creates good jobs, and lowers energy costs for surrounding communities. 03 Butler University: Infant Mortality in Indianapolis Problem: Indianapolis has one of the highest infant mortality rates among major American cities, and mothers on Medicaid too often miss the prenatal care and early warnings that save infant lives. Approach: The Institute is working with Butler University to use AI in two ways: identifying trends and risk factors across the city’s maternal health data, and deploying a targeted intervention that reaches mothers on Medicaid with the right care at the right time. Goal: Reduce infant mortality in Indianapolis, improve prenatal care engagement among Medicaid mothers, and create a model other cities can replicate. 04 Secretary Cardona: AI Career Pathways Problem: Low-income students often lack access to clear, personalized guidance about career pathways, training options, credentials, and the jobs available to them. Approach: The Institute is exploring an AI-powered career pathways pilot that helps students understand possible careers, the skills required, the education or training pathways available, and the steps needed to move from school to work. Goal: Give low-income students better access to personalized career guidance, increase awareness of high-opportunity jobs, and help schools and workforce partners connect students to real economic mobility.
